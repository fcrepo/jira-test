FCREPO-1802 External triplestores to enforce the authorization rules defined in the repository

TypeImprovement
StatusOpen
PriorityMajor
ReporterAndrew Woods
Created2015-10-28 13:46 UTC
Updated2017-11-22 00:27 UTC
Affects versionsFedora 4.4.0

Description

This task is to investigate (and document in the wiki) potential approaches for enforcing WebAC policies defined on F4 resources when being queried in an external triplestore.
Although general approaches may be available, this work may also be unique to each triplestore. The triplestores that have been tested with F4 are:

  • Fuseki
  • Sesame
  • BlazeGraph

Unknown, 2015-11-04 02:20 UTC

Nick Ruest That would be great. I keep coming back to the idea that the triplestore is, perhaps, not the place to impose access controls. At least not fine grained access controls. Of course, if you want to expose a public sparql endpoint, that wouldn't work; unless, of course, you only put public data there.

However, I wonder about the possiblities that a linked data fragments server could provide – these also seem to be more useful than full SPARQL endpoints. And in that regard, I have this idea that I've been unable to bury. And the idea is this – what if your triplestore had a basic inference engine (e.g. simple rdfs entailment) and your fedora repository is being synced to that triplestore. Then, with a slightly more sophisticated route than the current fcrepo-indexer-triplestore, you index the content to the triplestore. Then, for every fedora resource you re-query the triplestore to get all of the added inferencing. With that information (and with the information that Fedora provides w/r/t the access controls – Jared Whiklo is working on this ticket), you can pass those "enhanced" triples to a cassandra based database. There, you have a column family that specifically handles authZ, so you get authorization controls for every triple in the repo. Plus, it scales like nobody's fool.

I'm not entirely sure that it would work. However, in the last two months, I also haven't been able to come up with a good reason why it wouldn't work.
