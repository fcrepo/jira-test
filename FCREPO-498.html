A. Soroka, 2010-04-01 15:33 UTC

I may be being naive here, but isn't this the sort of work best handed off to the container itself? Is not connection pooling (and transaction management, if it comes to that) a service that containers are supposed to provide for their applications?

I know Fedora is tightly bound to Tomcat right now, and that may limit what can be relied on from the container, but that (hopefully) won't always be the case.

Chris Wilper, 2010-04-02 19:52 UTC

Although it is routinely tested with Tomcat, Fedora does not require the use of a particular servlet container in order to run. Keeping it as container-neutral as possible would be good unless there's a big overriding reason not to.

On the surface, relying on the container to provide connections does sound like the Right Thing to do, but would require different DB configuration steps depending on the container you decide to deploy Fedora in.

Another idea is to do a hybrid: Allow people to configure it via JNDI (container-provided) if they want, but also give them the option of doing it directly. Confluence actually does this: http://confluence.atlassian.com/display/DOC/Confluence+Setup+Guide#ConfluenceSetupGuide-4.CustomInstallation%3ADatabaseConfiguration
